How it works

Words are transcribed by sound, not by spelling. The Russian word for “helper” is written with one sound and spoken with another, so it never enters the list for the sound it appears to contain.

A purity gate then removes every word holding a sound this child has not mastered. If the gate cannot verify a word, it stops the build instead of printing it.

Each block on the sheet carries the manual it comes from, and that provenance is computed from the data rather than declared.

Decisions

A claim without a source is a defect

The generator built an exercise on an image the field uses everywhere — one sound taught as a snake's hiss. Checked against the manual that exercise cited, the two did not match: the book was right, the exercise was wrong. Rule since then — every methodical statement on a sheet carries its source, and one without a source counts as a defect, not a rough edge.

A summary of a user is not the user

A speech therapist described the format she needed. What went into the code was a summary of her words rather than the words. A week later the two were compared: the format in the code was not hers. User's words now live verbatim in a file of their own, and any reading of them sits beside it, never on top.

The model was wrong and the engine was right

Navigation rested on a believed rule: one session, one step of difficulty. Ten real lesson plans were then coded into a dataset of thirty-three task names, and not one worked that way. A therapist moves along a frontier — how far this child has come with this sound — building a run-up to it. The rule was dropped. The engine, tested against the same dataset, held.

The moat had a hole in it

The filter checks the word it intends to place. On a picture task the child names what they see, and published naming norms put 41% of children on a different name for a common picture — one with none of the target sound in it. Found before print, not after.

Every word list was re-checked by a pass that distrusted the first

A second review went through the dictionaries assuming the first was wrong. It found 114 defects: misplaced stress, transcriptions that followed spelling instead of speech, words whose plural quietly loses the target sound, and claims in the project's own reports that the data did not support.

Not published yet. The gate: any therapist from the group of ~150 can open a link and print a sheet. No sheet has been used with a child in a real session — everything so far is verified against literature and data, not practice.
