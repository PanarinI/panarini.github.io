Speech-therapy worksheet generator

in build

Printable material for sound practice, assembled for one specific child: every word is filtered against the sounds that child cannot say yet — the one thing a printed workbook can never do.

How it works

Words are transcribed by sound, not by spelling. A Russian word can be written with one consonant and spoken with another, so it never enters the list for the sound it appears to contain.

A purity gate then removes every word holding a sound this child has not mastered yet. If the gate cannot verify a word, it stops the build instead of printing it.

What it refuses to print

This project has almost nothing to show as a screenshot: what changes is not a screen but what the engine is allowed to say. So here it is by example — the same two cases that taught it to distrust rules.

A rule by word-ending is wrong more often than it is right. Asked to print “five chairs”, a rule that inflects by the tail of the word produces a word that does not exist. Out of sixteen words tested, it was wrong about fifteen.

The rule was switched off. Five hundred and seventy-nine words were then inflected by hand, twice and independently, and only the forms both passes agreed on were kept.

The suffix that makes a thing smaller also makes it a different thing. A diminutive is the friendliest exercise on the sheet — say it fondly — and the least safe to generate, because the same ending that shrinks one word renames another.

What the engine learned

Newest first. Each entry is the same shape: what it could not do, what it had to know first, and what it can do now.

2026-08-10Two exercises existed on paper and not in fact

Both were listed as working features. Neither could actually be built for almost any sheet, because the rule they stood on had been switched off two days earlier as unreliable, and the hand-made table that replaced it covered twenty-three words out of one thousand seven hundred. They were counted as existing without existing. After the tables were filled they build on twenty-eight sheets out of twenty-nine, and on fourteen out of twenty-nine — and the second number is not a defect, see below.

2026-08-10One exercise cannot be built for hissing sounds, and the reason is the language itself

The fond diminutive almost always carries a particular consonant, and that consonant is forbidden on a sheet for any hissing or whistling sound, because a child practising one will slide into the other. Sixteen words on such a sheet, forms exist for twelve, eight of those fall at the purity gate. No amount of engineering moves that number: it is a property of Russian, and the screen should say so plainly rather than report that the exercise “could not be assembled”.

2026-08-09Ten tests had never run, and the suite reported all green

A single line that tells the file to execute its tests was sitting in the middle of that file instead of at its end. Everything written below it — ten test functions, five of them other people's, including a check added the day before — was silently skipped. The suite printed a clean result the whole time. Moving one line took the count from 1224 checks to 1649, and all of them pass.

2026-08-08Verbs were kept out of sentences until the dictionary knew enough about them

Sentences on the sheet were frozen phrases: point at a picture and name it. Making them live needs verbs, and a verb cannot be placed without knowing whether its subject is animate and whether the action is finished — and neither can be derived from how the word ends. So the verbs waited while three properties were marked by hand across the dictionary. Then the sentences moved: from “here is a ram” to “an elk is swimming past”.

2026-08-08Three materials were teaching the wrong sound

A practice track for one voiced consonant printed syllables that, read aloud, come out as its voiceless twin — so a sheet made to train one sound was training its opposite. Two more materials had the same class of fault. All three came off the paper the day it was measured. The lesson is the project's rule now: what a letter looks like and what a mouth does with it are different facts, and only the second one is allowed to decide anything.

Not published yet. The gate: any therapist from the group of ~150 can open a link and print a sheet. No sheet has been used with a child in a real session — everything so far is verified against literature and data, not practice.
