Teacher's assistant

in build

A teacher does not see his own work from the outside. After a question he waits less than a second, although from three seconds on the answers get longer. He praises less often than he thinks. Counting this needs someone who is present for the whole lesson. A mentor comes once a year; an app you have to open is not looked at mid-lesson. This assistant stands on the desk and counts by itself.

These numbers are not new. Wait time after a question. The count of praise against reprimands. How many chances to answer a class gets in a minute. All of it was measured in education research decades ago, and the effect of a teacher tracking these numbers is known too. There is no software for it. The reason is in the task: these numbers describe the teacher himself, and taking them needs someone who watches the whole lesson. Teachers count their own praise with a hand tally counter on the wrist.

What it is meant to do

Five jobs, spread over a teacher's day. Two of them are built. The counting has been checked on recordings and has not run in a real lesson; the conversation after the lesson works as it is. The other three are described here as the plan.

COUNTS THE LESSONpauses after a question, how much of the lesson was speech, later praise against reprimands. The numbers go to the teacher and to no one elsesays nothing during it
PROMPTS OUT LOUD“seven minutes have passed.” A screen pulls the teacher's eyes to the board; a voice does not, so he keeps looking at the childrensound, not a screen
TALKS AFTER THE LESSONtwo questions about what fell apart, and the answer goes into memory. Speech in, speech out, the history kept between conversationsthis part works
SUMS UP THE WEEKa week of counting turns into a single suggestion for the next one, and into what is not visible from inside: “this is the third time you have put group work in the last seven minutes”one thing at a time
PREPARES THE MATERIALa page of the textbook and, back, activities for each stage of the lesson — chosen knowing this class and what did not work last timethe textbook simply lies there

Two bodies, one program

Everything above is sound coming in, speech going out and memory on a server. A phone does all of that, and on three counts it does it better. Everyone already has one. It is the faster computer. It lies closer to the teacher than an object on the desk, and its microphone, camera and speaker are already inside. What the robot can do beyond that is one thing: move, and turn towards whoever is speaking.

So the counting program takes sound from anywhere, not only from the robot, and the phone and the robot are two bodies for the same thing. One reason to keep the robot remains. An app you have to open stops being opened. Whether an object standing on the desk holds a habit better is not known. That is a guess, and comparing the two bodies is how it gets checked.

What works today

The robot is a ready-made Reachy Mini by Pollen Robotics, bought assembled. Inside: nine servo motors, four microphones, a speaker, a camera, a single-board computer, and the maker's own program that drives the motors. Out of the box it moves and runs apps from the maker's store. It does not hold a conversation. Three programs were written to change that.

CONVERSATIONtakes the sound from the microphone, sends it to the cloud model and plays the answer through the speaker. The laptop takes no part in this, and the model is used on a free tokeninside the robot
MEMORY AND SEARCHreads the transcript after a pause in the conversation and writes what matters into a text file; on request it searches the web and reads files on the laptop. This is the only part we pay foron the laptop
COUNTING THE LESSONtakes a recording or the microphone, marks where speech starts and stops, and measures the pauses between. Two counters read the same markings — pauses and rhythm — and a counter that needs word recognition attaches the same way. Checked on recordings with a known answer, not yet on a real lessonwritten 1 September

The parts are started and stopped by separate buttons, because they break separately: the robot can answer out loud while its motors are off. There is one dependency — the conversation program does not start when the motors are off.

What the memory actually holds

The memory is a plain text file the robot writes itself. After a pause in the conversation the second program reads the transcript, and what is worth keeping goes into the file. Nothing is summarised for show — these are lines from that file as they stand.

7 AUG“My antennas cross when I am thinking or waiting — confirmed by someone watching from outside.”a gesture explained

What is not built yet

THE PHONE BODYthe counting program already takes sound from anywhere, but the phone side — the screen with the eyes, the microphone, the voice — does not exist. Today there is one body, and it is the robotnothing written for it
WHO IS SPEAKINGthe counter measures the pause between one voice and the next, without knowing whether the teacher or a pupil spoke. In a staged lesson with one person these are the same thing; in a class they are notone speech flag for the whole room
A QUESTION FROM A STATEMENTthe three-second norm is about the pause after a question. Until the words are recognised, the counter reports the whole spread of pauses instead of that one number, and says so out loud when it reportsneeds word recognition
TELL A PERSON FROM THE ROOMbecause of that the direction is computed within 180° only, and a sound in front is the same as a sound behind. The camera will do this job insteadfour microphones in one row
NOT HEAR HIS OWN VOICEthe microphone and the speaker work at the same time, so his own speech gets into the recordingno echo cancellation
SPEAK LOUDERat maximum volume the amplifier's noise is heard along with the speech. Processing the sound made it less intelligible and was rolled back; louder means an external speaker88 % is the working level

Journal

1 Sepcounting

I wrote the program that counts pauses: how long passes between the teacher finishing and the next voice starting. I have not run it on a real lesson yet. I checked it on a recording I made myself, with the answer known in advance: forty utterances, pauses from 0.4 to 0.9 seconds, six long ones. The program found ten utterances instead of forty. The threshold by which it decided an utterance had ended stood at three quarters of a second. It glued neighbouring utterances together and erased the short pauses the whole thing was written for. A teacher who never waits would have looked exemplary. I set the threshold to a quarter of a second and the rate to twenty readings per second. On the same recording it now gives forty utterances and a median of 0.6, against the 0.66 that was planted. The price: a pause shorter than a third of a second is not told apart from a breath in the middle of a phrase.

1 Sepbodies

I had been counting on the robot being necessary. I went through every scene I had described. None of them needs it. Sound in, speech out, memory on a server — a phone does all of that, and better on three counts. What the robot has beyond a phone came to three things, and two of them turned out weaker than they looked. It reports the direction a sound came from, but only within 180°, so front and back are the same. It has a camera that stands in the room, while a phone sits in a pocket. But a phone on a tripod stands too. What is left is movement. So I split the program: the counter takes sound from anywhere, and the robot became one body out of two.

10 Augdocumentation

We built a test rig to work out the direction a voice comes from and recorded twelve scenes. It turned out that the robot does not tell a sound in front from a sound behind. This is written in the maker's documentation: the four microphones sit in one row, so the direction is computed within 180° only. Half a day went into checking a known fact. Since then a new task starts with reading the documentation and the existing apps, and measurements are made only where there is no data.

9 Augsearch

When Richie was asked something he had to look up, he went silent for the whole search. The program inside the robot was waiting for the laptop and could not finish the reply. Now the request is confirmed at once. Richie says that he is searching and goes on talking, and the result arrives a few seconds later as a separate line. The cost of this: the answer is not attached to the question and can arrive when the conversation has moved on.

9 Augcost

Requests to the model cost 10 dollars in one day. One question was processed by six requests in a row. Each of them sent the whole previous conversation together with the text of the pages found. The longer the conversation, the more every next request costs. We split the jobs — search goes to the more expensive model, sorting the conversation into memory goes to the cheap one. Every answer now prints its price. After the switch the cheap model turned out not to accept one of the parameters, and memory quietly stopped being written for a day.

7 Augmemory

Richie was reading the project files, the ones where we write down the work and the breakdowns. He was telling the author about faults he no longer had. We made separate files for the robot itself: who he is, what works right now, why things are done this way. He reads them before every answer. The cost: we update these files by hand, he does not keep them himself.

6 Augmicrophone

The microphone hands over the recording in two channels. We were joining them into one row, one after the other. The recording played twice as fast, and speech recognition returned fragments or nothing at all. The level and the spectrum looked normal, so the error was invisible on the instruments. We started taking the average of the two channels: speech was recognised, and the background noise we had taken for the motors dropped elevenfold.

4 Augcontrol

Controlling the robot through the maker's library did not work. It drops the connection if the robot does not respond for a second, and the robot does not respond while it is busy. Its video channel hangs the whole interface. We went to the service program inside the robot directly: it has a web interface of its own, and a movement command runs in 23 milliseconds. What we take from this: if a device comes with a convenient wrapper, it is worth looking at what is under it.

4 Augassembly

The robot was ordered in July and arrived in a box; assembly took one evening. Out of the box it moves, and apps from the maker's store run on it. We ran three of them to see what the platform can do. None of them makes a conversation that keeps its history, so from here on the work is our own programs.

The robot stands at home, the programs written for it are not published and the source code is closed. It has never been in a classroom, and the counting program has so far only been run on recordings I made myself. The phone body does not exist yet. This page says what the thing is for and which part of it runs today.
