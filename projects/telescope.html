Demand Telescope

in use

An instrument for deciding what to build next. Search demand becomes a sky you can fly through: every query a star, its size the monthly volume, its colour how crowded that door already is.

What it looked like

Read the counts, not the density. The view fits itself to whatever it holds, so the sky looks emptier as it fills: seven times more stars in the last frame than in the first, drawn seven times smaller. Two earlier frames exist and stay unpublished — on those the instrument's own method is legible, and this page shows what the sky sees, not how it measures.

How it works

Every query the instrument has ever looked at stays as a node. Size is monthly volume; colour is how occupied that door already is; edges are relations found in the data.

Positions are not decided by hand and not simulated in the browser. The layout is computed offline from the relations themselves, so a sky of a thousand-odd nodes stays smooth to fly through, with detail appearing as you descend. Dragging a node is therefore reading, not editing — a reset puts it back where the data says it belongs.

Regions are the questions that were asked, not properties of the data — which is why every run, on any topic, pours into one sky rather than into a folder of separate maps.

Decision log

Newest first. A decision appears here only if a reader with no context can follow it to the end without once asking “what is that?” — the rest stay in the project’s own log.

2026-07-14Nothing is drawn that the map’s own dictionary cannot define

A small star meaning “the author’s intuition pulls here” had survived three migrations of the dataset and was still on the map, defined nowhere. It came off. The rule since: every mark carries a meaning written down in the map’s dictionary, or it is not drawn — and that dictionary sits on the map itself, one keypress away, formulas included.

2026-07-14The interpretation layer was removed entirely, and the map became documentation

Fields, axes and bridges were a fossil of the first week: a reading laid over the data and then treated as though it were the data. All of it was archived at once and the dome rebuilt layer by layer, each layer stating how well it is proved — fact of the text, waiting for judgement, or judged. Every star now says what is not known about it, so the map's silence stops reading as a verdict.

2026-07-14The frame of a run was pulled out of the layout

Each run carried the name of the idea it was asked around, and that name was holding nodes together in space. But “turn based games” is not, lexically, a new-tab query: the frame of the question was freezing into the structure of the answer. It became a layer you can switch off. What holds positions now is only what is in the data — and where the data holds nothing, the map says so: the relative position of two islands means nothing at all.

2026-07-14Spelling is not meaning

Relatives were drifting alone — “multiplication worksheets grade 4” far from “multiplication worksheet” — because the rule that builds the skeleton read exact words, and to a machine “worksheet” and “worksheets” are two different things. Plurals and hyphens were declared spelling and glued; differences of meaning are still settled only by evidence. Roots fell from 81 to 66 and forty-nine pairs left the queue without a single query being bought.

2026-07-13Structure is read from the text of the phrases, not from what the search engine suggests

Five attempts at grouping the sky failed in a row, every one of them caught by eye rather than by a metric. What survived is the single structure that can be derived without argument: a longer phrase contains a shorter one. The list of related queries turns out to mix two different things — the tails of a phrase and its genuine relatives — and the search engine does not distinguish them, so it cannot be drawn as though it were structure.

2026-07-12A star and a constellation are not the same kind of object

Measured queries and our own groupings were drawn identically, as circles with a size. But a query exists whether or not anyone looked at it, while a grouping is a figure a person draws between stars and has no measurements of its own. Drawing the second like the first gave it a volume it never had — a lie told in the visual language rather than in words.

2026-07-12Two ceilings were removed by architecture, not by changing technology

The map stalled twice over: the browser was recomputing the physics every frame, and it was drawing everything whether or not it was on screen. The obvious answer was a graphics layer built for thousands of objects. Instead the layout moved out of the browser altogether and only what is inside the camera gets drawn. The obvious answer would have cost hover and click — and pointing at a star to read it is the entire purpose of the instrument.

2026-07-12Computing the groupings was tried, and it failed honestly

Grouping the sky automatically from the graph of related queries was the attractive answer: computed beats assigned. Run on real data it produced families of a single word — a theme with its variants — and nothing larger. Worksheets and flashcards are both a teacher's tools, but no search engine has ever linked them, so no algorithm over those links can see it. Related catches vocabulary, not meaning. The larger groupings are still drawn by hand, and labelled as drawn by hand.

What the instrument sees is shown here. How it measures — where the numbers come from, how crowding is scored, and which questions are being explored right now — stays off this page.
