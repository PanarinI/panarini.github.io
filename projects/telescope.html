Demand Telescope

in use

An instrument for deciding what to build next. Search demand becomes a sky you can fly through: every query a star, its size the monthly volume, its colour how crowded that door already is.

What it looked like

Read the counts, not the density. The view fits itself to whatever it holds, so the sky looks emptier as it fills: seven times more stars in the last frame than in the first, drawn seven times smaller. Two earlier frames exist and stay unpublished — on those the instrument's own method is legible, and this page shows what the sky sees, not how it measures.

How it works

Positions are not decided by hand and not simulated in the browser: the layout is computed offline from the relations themselves, so a sky of a thousand-odd nodes stays smooth to fly through. Dragging a node is therefore reading, not editing — a reset puts it back where the data says it belongs, and the map gets corrected through its data, never with the mouse.

Those three numbers belong together on purpose: the instrument may only claim what it has measured, so the count of what it has not reached yet is part of the reading.

Decision log

Newest first. A decision appears here only if a reader with no context can follow it to the end without once asking “what is that?” — the rest stay in the project’s own log.

2026-07-20A node's card is a table, never a sentence

Hovering a star used to produce explanatory phrases — “enough for a narrow niche”. One of them turned out to be plainly wrong, because the thing it asserted cannot be derived from the number it sat next to. Prose in a cell blends a measurement with an interpretation of it, and the blend hides which part is which. The card became a table of parameter and value, with an explicit “no data” where there is none, and every interpretation moved into the map's dictionary where it can carry a confidence mark.

2026-07-19A competitor counts for a query only if it is on the first page for that query

Products were being attached to queries because they lived somewhere in the same subject. But the first page of results is the only place where a demand and a product actually meet; below it, the product is invisible to the person searching, and the link between them is a guess. Anything further down was dropped, and products whose earnings are unknown were split into a line of their own rather than counted in.

2026-07-19A name is taken only when it matches exactly

Occupancy of a name was being marked red whenever the words appeared anywhere in a competitor's title. Checked by hand against the store, that was simply wrong: registration is blocked by an exact match, and extra words around a phrase dilute it rather than claim it. The colour was rebuilt around that distinction — and the “free” state is drawn as a ring rather than a solid mark, because a public catalogue cannot see names that are reserved but unpublished.

2026-07-17Occupancy is decided by where a phrase sits in a name, not by whether the words appear

A title that contains the words of a query scattered among others is not competing for that query; a title that opens with the phrase is. The rule was rewritten around the position of the exact phrase, and the census moved from a general search engine to the store's own search, which is where the person looking for a product actually looks.

2026-07-17A borrowed word was given back its original meaning

Two different things were sharing one term: an open-source project whose code gets reused, and a competitor with revenue. The dataset had grown a note explaining which was meant where — a sure sign the word was doing two jobs. They were split and renamed, and the note became unnecessary.

2026-07-15The map counts its own coverage, out loud

A line of counters was put under the title: how many stars, how much demand they add up to, how many have been checked against real results, how many pairs are still waiting. The first reading was uncomfortable and therefore useful — most of the sky had not been measured. A map that shows only what it knows reads as complete; this one shows the size of what it does not.

2026-07-14Nothing is drawn that the map's own dictionary cannot define

A small star meaning “the author's intuition pulls here” had survived three migrations of the dataset and was still on the map, defined nowhere. It came off. The rule since: every mark carries a meaning written down in the map's dictionary, or it is not drawn — and that dictionary sits on the map itself, one keypress away.

2026-07-14The interpretation layer was removed entirely, and the map became documentation

Fields, axes and bridges were a fossil of the first week: a reading laid over the data and then treated as though it were the data. All of it was archived at once and the dome rebuilt layer by layer, each layer stating how well it is proved. Every star now says what is not known about it, so the map's silence stops reading as a verdict.

2026-07-14The frame of a run was pulled out of the layout

Each run carried the name of the idea it was asked around, and that name was holding nodes together in space. But “turn based games” is not, lexically, a new-tab query: the frame of the question was freezing into the structure of the answer. It became a layer you can switch off. What holds positions now is only what is in the data — and where the data holds nothing, the map says so: the relative position of two islands means nothing at all.

2026-07-14Spelling is not meaning

Relatives were drifting alone — “multiplication worksheets grade 4” far from “multiplication worksheet” — because the rule that builds the skeleton read exact words, and to a machine “worksheet” and “worksheets” are two different things. Plurals and hyphens were declared spelling and glued; differences of meaning are still settled only by evidence. Roots fell from 81 to 66 and forty-nine pairs left the queue without a single query being bought.

2026-07-13Structure is read from the text of the phrases, not from what the search engine suggests

Five attempts at grouping the sky failed in a row, every one caught by eye rather than by a metric. What survived is the single structure that can be derived without argument: a longer phrase contains a shorter one. The list of related queries mixes two different things — the tails of a phrase and its genuine relatives — and the search engine does not distinguish them, so it cannot be drawn as though it were structure.

2026-07-12Collecting and drawing were split along one seam

Research and rendering had been one process, so neither could move while the other was open. They were separated by file: research writes data, rendering is code, and the map is simply a function of the two. A written contract for the data is the only place they touch. Since then a run in one session and a rewrite of the map in another have not once collided.

2026-07-12A star and a constellation are not the same kind of object

Measured queries and our own groupings were drawn identically, as circles with a size. But a query exists whether or not anyone looked at it, while a grouping is a figure a person draws between stars and has no measurements of its own. Drawing the second like the first gave it a volume it never had — a lie told in the visual language rather than in words.

2026-07-12Two ceilings were removed by architecture, not by changing technology

The map stalled twice over: the browser was recomputing the physics every frame, and drawing everything whether or not it was on screen. The obvious answer was a graphics layer built for thousands of objects. Instead the layout moved out of the browser altogether and only what is inside the camera gets drawn. The obvious answer would have cost hover and click — and pointing at a star to read it is the entire purpose of the instrument.

2026-07-12Computing the groupings was tried, and it failed honestly

Grouping the sky automatically from the graph of related queries was the attractive answer: computed beats assigned. Run on real data it produced families of a single word — a theme with its variants — and nothing larger. Worksheets and flashcards are both a teacher's tools, but no search engine has ever linked them, so no algorithm over those links can see it. The larger groupings are still drawn by hand, and labelled as drawn by hand.

2026-07-11One sky, instead of a file per topic

Every piece of research had been producing its own map, which made the maps incomparable and threw away the most interesting thing — where two subjects touch. One dataset was declared instead, with the topic of a run kept as a tag rather than a folder. The first thing it showed was a bridge nobody had asked about: a query that belongs to two subjects at once.

What the instrument sees is shown here. How it measures — where the numbers come from, how crowding is scored, and which questions are being explored right now — stays off this page.
